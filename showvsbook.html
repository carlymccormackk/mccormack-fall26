Major Differences Between the Book Series and Show

  1. Justin’s character: In The Deal, Justin is a football player. Hannah has a crush on him because of his intelligence and difference from stereotypical athletes. In the show, Justin is a musician. This connects him more to Hannah because of her personal interest in music.
  2. Hannah’s musical storyline: In The Deal, Hannah is a music major and singer-songwriter. She is involved in the Pop-Music Showcase and works with a classmate, Cass. In the show, Cass is removed and Justin becomes involved in Hannah’s music. The show also makes Hannah’s musical future more important by making her scholarship dependent on the Pop-Music Showcase.
  3. Hannah’s jobs: In The Deal, Hannah works at Della’s, a ‘50’s themes diner, as well as Malones. In the show, Hannah works at the Briar hockey rink and Malones, which is changed from being a bar the hockey players visit into a restaurant where Hannah and Allie work.
  4. Dean and Allie: In The Deal, the main story is Hannah and Garrett. Dean and Allie’s romance doesn’t happen until the third book of the series, The Score. The show moves their storyline forward by developing Allie and Dean’s relationship during Season 1.
  5. Hannah’s Attacker: In The Deal, Hannah’s attacker doesn’t appear directly, instead she has an interaction with Rob Delaney, a friend of the attacker, who lied in court about what happened. In the show, they made Rob Delaney himself as Hannah’s attacker and making him a hockey player from a rival school St. Anthony’s. This allows the show to have a more direct conflict between Garrett and Hannah’s past.
  6. Garrett and Hannah’s Breakup: In The Deal, Phil Graham is responsible for the breakup between Garrett and Hannah. He threatened to cut Garrett off financially if Hannah didn’t break up with him, so she secretly broke up with him since she was worried about ruining his future. Garrett discovered the reason and confronted his father about it. In the show, Garrett breaks up with Hannah himself. He fought Hannah’s attacker during their hockey game, making him scared by his own violence. When Hannah told him his behavior scared her, he ended the relationship, worried that he might end up like his father.
  7. The “hands-off” rule: In The Deal, Garrett actually uses a campus-wide “hands-off” rule as a part of his attempt to get Hannah back after their breakup. In the show, the rule is mentioned, but it was essentially a rumor started by the younger hockey team players.
